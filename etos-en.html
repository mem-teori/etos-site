Core architecture · v5.6.0

Six layers, one partially closed governance loop

In v5.6.0, every decision moves from constitutional boundaries through the decision engine and human oversight to reflection, governance, and a traceable audit trail.

01 · Foundation

M.E.M. and CDP

M.E.M. supplies the relational and tension framing. CDP supplies constitutional boundaries that cannot be overridden.

02 · Decision layer

Decision Engine

Normalises tension dimensions and calculates divergence, recoverability, and ethical debt. Every decision receives one of five modes: Normal, Requires Review, Emergency, Incompatible, or Constitutional Veto.

03 · Human oversight

Human review

Every non-Normal mode requires human review. A review creates a lifecycle transition and an audit receipt. Approval is a governance decision — not automatic execution of a domain action.

04 · Reflection

Reflection Center

Aggregates modes, divergence, ethical debt, and attention over time, making cross-decision patterns visible.

05 · Governance Board Beta

Active regulator

Evaluates cross-case patterns and proposed system changes. Outputs are approve, review, pause, or veto — within the constitutional boundaries, not above them.

06 · Provenance

SHA-256 audit chain

Every governance-relevant action can be written to a SHA-256 hash chain. LRE and the M.E.M.I. lifecycle are preserved as separate evidence and integration layers rather than silently fused.

Key concepts
T

Tensions

Decisions are analysed as tensions across five dimensions: risk, coercion, capacity, time, and uncertainty. Each dimension’s contribution is shown, so it is visible what drove the outcome.

D

Divergence & recoverability

The combined weighted tension (divergence) determines the decision mode. Recoverability shows how easily consequences can be corrected and falls as divergence rises.

E

Ethical Debt

The durable consequences of decisions. Ethical debt is recorded as events, so its history is preserved even when the current balance is resolved.

G

Governance Modes

Normal, Requires Review, Emergency, Incompatible, and Constitutional Veto. Governance is not documentation afterward — it is an operational mode within the system.

EU AI Act · support

ETOS is developed to support work with the EU AI Act —
architecturally aligned with the requirements from day one

The EU AI Act requires explainability, human oversight, risk management, and auditability for high-risk AI systems. ETOS is developed to support work with these requirements; it is not in itself a certification or conformity assessment.

Art. 13 · Transparency

Explainability is structural

ETOS generates a decision trace for every decision: which tensions were active, what governance state the system was in, and what influenced the outcome. Not as a log after the fact — as output within the process.

Art. 14 · Human Oversight

Human control is enforceable

In ETOS v5.6.0, every non-Normal mode requires human review — an operational mechanism, not a recommendation. Positive review, evidence verification, and release authorisation are role-separated, and the Constitutional Veto cannot be overridden.

Art. 9 · Risk Management

Risk is continuous, not point-in-time

Divergence, recoverability, and standing ethical debt are assessed for every decision, and the Reflection Center and Governance Board follow patterns over time. ETOS supports the continuous risk management requirement — a formal risk plan, testing evidence, and residual-risk acceptance must still be supplied for the specific system.

Art. 12 · Record-keeping

Historical accountability as a core layer

Decisions, reviews, and lifecycle transitions are stored in the database and in a SHA-256 hash chain. Compliance evidence is stored as append-only, hash-chained receipts bound to a specific decision and version. Auditability is not an add-on — it is one of the architectural layers.

Art. 15 · Accuracy & Robustness

Measurement evidence, not claims

Robustness must be documented with observed results, datasets and subgroups, method, evaluator, model version, and evidence references — not only metrics and thresholds. ETOS gates on missing measurement evidence but does not itself generate the underlying test result.

Overall assessment

Architecturally aligned, not certified

ETOS is not certified as EU AI Act-compliant. It is designed around the structural requirements the Act attempts to codify: transparency, human oversight, risk management, record-keeping, and robustness.

New in v5.6.0

What the frozen v5.6.0 adds

v5.6.0 does not claim that ETOS itself performs the legal, organisational, or scientific acts that a provider, deployer, assessor, or authority must perform. Instead, it makes those boundaries explicit and fail-closed.

Safety invariants

One canonical rule set

A canonical registry of safety rules: no autonomous real-world execution, no hack-back or destructive response, no self-approval. Any missing invariant or attempted loosening blocks release (fail-closed).

External evidence

What ETOS cannot create itself

A registry for evidence ETOS cannot truthfully create on its own. Registration distinguishes “ready for submission” from confirmed registration — without a database reference and confirmation, ETOS cannot claim the system is registered.

Data and documentation

Art. 10 and Annex IV

Datasets require evidence of provenance, representativeness, quality, bias testing, and more before they pass. Technical documentation is expanded along Annex IV, and missing information remains an explicit gap.

Measurement and quality

Art. 15 and 17

An explicit Art. 15 boundary: ETOS requires supplied measurement evidence but does not generate the result itself. An expanded technical control register for quality management (QMS) and a formal post-market monitoring plan.

Humans in the FRIA

Art. 27

The fundamental rights impact assessment is a human workflow: a human assessor, an independent reviewer, and sign-off are required. ETOS does not autonomously determine the impact.

Release status

Three states

BLOCKED, EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED, or READY_WITHIN_ETOS_CONTROL_BOUNDARY. The last means ETOS’s own controls have passed — not legal certification. Plus a hardened boundary between immutable, hash-verified files and runtime data.

The boundary: ETOS COMPLETE v5.6.0 is a technical governance/compliance-support reference architecture. Its frozen status establishes the integrity and tested behaviour of this release; it is not EU AI Act certification, a conformity assessment, a declaration of conformity, a CE mark, EU database registration, deployment-specific compliance evidence, or a legal opinion.

Security module

Security that can only tighten

The security adapter is part of the tested core. It maps suspicious events into a deterministic risk signal that can only tighten governance — it can never loosen it or grant authority to act.

“It plans/signals; it never executes.” — from the adapter’s source code

Tested core

Security adapter

The signal is fed into the Decision Engine alongside the other tensions. The v5.6.0 tests check that the mapping is deterministic and non-executing.

Reference

Defense / APEX

The Defense and APEX code is included as a reference implementation and provenance. It has no runtime authority in the ETOS core.

What makes ETOS different

The system regulates its own evolution through governance

ETOS treats decisions as tensions rather than answers. It learns under governance rather than around it. And it can explain what it does, why it does it, and how it changes over time.
